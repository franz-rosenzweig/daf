# Daf for the Mac

This is Daf for the Mac made the way a Mac app is made: SwiftUI and AppKit, with the system's own window, sidebar, toolbar, search, inspector and Settings, instead of the web page in a window. It began beside the web-view app (`mac/`) as "Daf Native" so I could try both; since 0.3.0 it *is* Daf (`xyz.tiresia.daf`), in its place in /Applications, with everything the web-view app did for me: Save to Daf in the share menu, Daf for Safari, alerts for new posts, today's minutes in the menu bar, and reading with no connection. The first time it opens it takes the web-view app's address, menu bar choice and sign-in, so there's nothing to set up.

It talks to Daf the same way the page does, through Daf's own API, so nothing changes on the server and everything I do in it shows up everywhere else.

## What's native

- **The window.** The sidebar has Today and Review; the Library's Inbox, Later, Shortlist and Archive; the Feed, with its folders opening onto their feeds, each with what's unseen in it; Books, Manga, Torah, Podcasts, Highlights and Stats; and Sources. Search sits at the top of the sidebar. The Go menu has them all (⌘1 to ⌘8 for the main ones).
- **Today.** The minutes ring with the main goal, Year 30 so far, the week as a chart against the goal, today's reading, and what to keep reading.
- **Home.** The Library's shelves, as on the page: continue reading, recently added, quick reads, long reads, recently highlighted, new in the Feed, each one shown or hidden.
- **Lists.** The Library and the Feed, with pictures, minutes and progress. Right-click (or the toolbar) for Archive, Read Later, Mark Seen, Save to Library, Open the Original, Send to Kindle and Delete. Delete has Undo, in the little note at the bottom and in Edit › Undo (⌘Z). ⌘-click or Shift-click to choose several and archive, move, mark, send or delete them all at once, with Undo putting each back where it was. A double-click opens something in a window of its own.
- **Order and filters.** The toolbar's filter button has the page's orders (newest, oldest, published, shortest, longest, started first) and filters (length, when it came, highlighted, not started, and a tag in the Library). Save as View… keeps the list, with its order and filters, under Views in the sidebar, here and in the page.
- **The Feed's sources.** Right-click a feed to rename it, move it to another folder, turn on "Always load the full article" or "Tell me about new posts", copy its address or unfollow it. Mark All Seen (⌥⌘K) works on the whole Feed, a folder or one feed, with Undo. Check for New Posts (⇧⌘R) asks FreshRSS now, and Feed › Follow a Site… adds one.
- **The article reader.** Native text in a column, the way Apple Books lays it out: headings, lists, quotes, pictures and captions, my highlights in their colours. Select and press **H** (or right-click → Highlight) to highlight. Right-click a highlight to give it a note or another colour, or to remove it; a highlight with a note is underlined with dots, and the note shows when I hold the pointer over it. It keeps my place in Daf and counts my reading time exactly as the page does (15 seconds at a time, while I'm actually reading).
- **A highlight's tools**, in the article, a book and Highlights alike: right-click one for its note, tags, colour, favorite, flashcards (fill in the blank or a question, or Daf suggests one), Copy, Share as Image (a card to send) and Remove (with Undo). Selecting words highlights them, as on the page (hold ⌥ to select without; Article › Highlight When Selecting turns it off); a highlight over another merges them. Right-click a word to Define it, or a Hebrew phrase to Translate it, with "In This Sentence" for what it means there.
- **The article's own tools**: the Shortlist (F), the next and previous in the list (⇧J and ⇧K, or ⌥⌘↓ and ⌥⌘↑), Summarize in the inspector, Load the Full Article for a feed that sends only a summary, Edit the Title and Author, Reset Reading Progress, Copy the Link, Open the Note in Obsidian. J and K move a focus from paragraph to paragraph; H and N highlight the one in focus when nothing is selected. Archive, Later and Delete have Undo (Z).
- **The inspector** (⌥⌘I) has the article's details, my note on it, its tags (Article › Tags…, ⌥⌘T, with the tags Daf already knows as suggestions), the summary, Ask and the highlights. PDFs and videos have the note and the tags too.
- **Ask** (⌥⌘A) answers a question about the article, with Daf's suggested questions to start; select a passage and right-click → Ask About This Passage to ask what it means there. The answers stay with the article.
- **Read aloud.** **P** listens or pauses (⌥⌘P from the menu), **L** or right-click → Read Aloud from Here starts at a paragraph. It's Kokoro on Daf, a paragraph at a time, the next one made while this one plays; the paragraph being read is marked and kept in view, and the player at the bottom has the paragraph before and after, the speed and Stop. The keyboard's play and skip keys work too. Listening counts as reading. The voice is in Settings.
- **Review.** Today's highlights one at a time, with Keep, Less, Never, a note and Favorite, and the flashcards that are due, as in the page (K, L, N and F, the arrow keys, Space, and 1 to 4 for a card).
- **Highlights.** Every book and article with highlights; each one's highlights with their notes, colours and tags. Open at Its Spot opens the article in a window of its own at that highlight.
- **Stats.** The numbers, the last two weeks against the goal, where the minutes come from, highlights a week, the hours and the days of the week, in Apple's charts.
- **The book reader.** EPUBs open natively, the way Apple Books sets a book on the Mac: a chapter at a time as pages, two side by side when the window is wide, turned with the arrow keys, Space, a swipe or a click at either edge, right to left for a Hebrew book. The contents, my highlights and my bookmarks sit in the sidebar (⌥⌘T); the Aa button has the theme (Original, Paper, Quiet, Night, or with the Mac), the typeface, the size, the line spacing and justified text. **H** highlights the selection, **B** bookmarks the page, and right-click a highlight for its note, its colour or to remove it. A footnote opens where its number is. ⌘F searches the whole book. **P** reads it aloud from the page I'm on (**L**, or right-click → Read Aloud from Here, from a paragraph), turning the pages and going on into the next chapter. My Kindle highlights are on the pages too, found in this edition by their words, and listed in the sidebar under From the Kindle. Ask, in the sidebar or by right-clicking a passage, answers a question about the book with the pages I'm on as its context. My place, highlights and bookmarks are kept exactly as the page keeps them (the same CFIs), so the page's reader, this one and the Kindle agree; when the Kindle is further on, it says so and offers to go there. Reading time and pages count as on the page. Other formats (MOBI, AZW3, FB2, comics) open in Daf's own reader.
- **Books.** A book's own page: its cover, where I am, the nightstand, its shelves, my reading of it (time, sittings, pace, when I'd finish) and its highlights. Read opens it in a window of its own. Books opens onto my shelves in the sidebar; right-click for New Shelf…, Rename… and Delete the Shelf (a Hardcover list stays, as on the page).
- **Manga.** A series' volumes as covers. A volume opens in a reader with everything the page's has: right to left, left to right, vertical, or as one long strip (with or without gaps); one page or two, pairs shifted by one, wide pages split; fit to the screen, the width or the height, or at its own size; borders cropped; the background and the filters. Each series keeps its own way of reading, in Daf, so the page and the phone read it the same. The trackpad's swipe, the wheel, the arrow keys, Space or the tap zones turn; pinch or double-click zooms. The bars (M, or a click in the middle) have the page slider, the volumes on either side and the quick settings; T shows every page, S the settings, A scrolls by itself, F is full screen. The start and end cards lead to the volume before and the next one.
- **Torah, Podcasts and Sources.** This week's parasha and haftarah with the readings, which open in the reader; what I've listened to lately and what I follow; whether each place Daf reads from is answering, with Sync Now.
- **PDFs** open in Apple's own PDF view.
- **Paper pages.** A photo of a page I marked in a paper book (File › Add a Photo of a Page…, File › Import from iPhone › Take Photo, or dropped on the list) goes to Daf, which reads its lines and the marks on them. I pick the book and the lines beside the photo, and each run of lines becomes a highlight in the book's note.
- **Settings** (⌘,) has the address and sign-in, today's minutes in the menu bar, the alerts, what's kept for no connection, Daf for Safari, and Daf's reading preferences: highlights and flashcards a day, how long unseen Feed items stay, the voice and the speed for reading aloud. Goals has every goal (on or off, its target, its period, the one the ring follows), the presets, and what counts toward the reading time.
- **File menu.** Add a Link… (⌘L) and Log Paper Minutes… (⇧⌘P).
- **Save to Daf** in the share menu of Safari and every other app puts the link in the Library (it hands it to the app as `daf://save?url=…`); `daf://go/review` and the like open a place, for Shortcuts.
- **Daf for Safari** (Settings, or the Daf menu): the app makes the extension's token with my sign-in and hands it over, then opens Safari's settings to switch it on.
- **Alerts for new posts** in the feeds I choose (right-click a feed → Tell Me About New Posts), every five minutes, as Mac notifications; a click opens the post.
- **With no connection** the lists, articles and books I've opened, the Inbox's and Later's articles and the nightstand's books open from what this Mac kept, and what I change (highlights, notes, places, reading time, archiving) waits on the Mac and goes to Daf when it answers, each change once. The sidebar says when it's offline and how many changes are waiting.

## What's still the page

Videos and podcast episodes open as Daf's own pages (without the page's sidebar), and so do books that aren't EPUBs. They count reading time and keep their place themselves, as in a browser.

## Not yet

Photos of paper pages, and the rest of Daf's preferences, which open as the page from Settings.

## Building it

On the Mac:

    cd mac-native && ./build.sh

That makes `build/Daf.app` with Save to Daf and Daf for Safari inside (both from `mac/DafShare` and `mac/DafSafari`, unchanged), signed with my Apple Development identity when the keychain is open (over SSH it isn't; then it's signed ad hoc, and Safari won't load its extension). Copy it to /Applications.

`./selftest.sh BASE COOKIE TOKEN` builds "Daf Test" (`xyz.tiresia.daf.selftest`, its own settings, sign-in and files, so Daf's are never touched) and runs it against a sandbox Daf, never the real one. It writes `build/selftest/log.txt` with a picture of each step, then clears what the test app kept. A tailnet sandbox needs macOS's local-network permission for the app; an SSH tunnel to the Mac's own 127.0.0.1 avoids that. Reading aloud plays silently in the test, and no question goes to the model.

The web-view Daf 0.2.1 is kept, zipped, in case I want it back.
