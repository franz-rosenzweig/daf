# How I use it

This page is my ordinary day with Daf, from the first highlight in the morning to the last page at night, so you can see how the pieces fit together before you read about each one. I built Daf for myself, and everything here is how I actually use it.

## Before six

At 05:32, Sunday to Friday, my Signal bot, Ezer, sends me the morning highlight: one passage from the day's review, with its book and a link to the rest. Most mornings I just read it, and that's the point, because it's something I once thought was worth keeping and had completely forgotten. When it sparks something, I reply "n:" and a thought, and the note goes under that highlight in my vault.

Later in the morning I open **Review** and go through the other four. I press **Keep** for the ones that still matter, and they come back in a few days, then less and less often. **Less** sends one far away, and **Never** takes it out of the review for good. It takes about two minutes, and on the busy days it's the one piece of reading I'm sure to do.

## During the day

I work at my desk on the Mac, and the menu bar shows today's minutes all day: "38′" until the hour is done, then "60′ ✓". It's a small thing, but having it in sight is half of what Year 30 needed.

When I find an article worth reading, I don't read it right then. I select a line and press Highlight in the browser extension, which saves the page to the Library with the highlight already on it, or I use "Save to Daf" from the share menu, and it waits for me in the Library's **Inbox**. The next morning at seven, the morning email lists what I saved, each with a two- or three-line summary.

The Feed is where my RSS lives now, instead of Read You. I read it a folder at a time, and anything I don't get to moves to **Seen** after a month, so the Feed never turns into a pile I feel guilty about. When a post deserves more than a skim, I highlight it, which saves it to the Library on its own.

## In the evening

Evenings are for books. I read on the Kindle in KOReader, and the Daf plugin sends the page turns whenever the Kindle sleeps, so by the time I look at Today the minutes are already there. The sleep screen tells me where I am without opening anything: how many of my sixty minutes I've read, and which day of Year 30 it is.

When I'm at the desk instead, I read the same book in Daf. If the Kindle is further along, Daf asks whether to go there, and when I go back to the Kindle, it asks the same about Daf, so I never hunt for my place. A voice note to Ezer while I'm reading goes under the book I'm on, at the page I'm on.

On the phone, the widget sits on my first screen, right where Instagram and Reddit are. Late in the evening, if I open one of those apps anyway, Daf can send one highlight and the minutes I have left. It doesn't block anything, it just reminds me what I said I wanted.

## Shabbat

On Friday, Today reminds me that Shabbat is coming and that the week's parasha is on the **Torah shelf**. I read it there before Shabbat, Hebrew beside the English, along with Rabbi Sacks' Covenant & Conversation essays for that week.

On Shabbat itself I read paper books, and I mark the pages I want to keep in pencil. After havdalah I text Ezer "shabbat 60", and the hour goes on Saturday, where it belongs. Then I photograph the pages I marked and send them too, and Daf reads the lines and turns the marked ones into highlights in that book.

## What I look at, and what I don't

I look at Today and the review every day. I look at **Stats** now and then, mostly the streak and "Waiting for you", which lists the books I started and left, because one sitting is often all it takes to get one moving again.

I don't fiddle with settings much. The goal is an hour a day, and the **Year 30** preset in Preferences does the rest. Some books I keep private: they count toward the hour, but their titles never show up in the review, the morning message, the widget or on the Kindle.

Basically, Daf isn't another app I have to remember to open. It sits in the places I already am, the Kindle, the phone, the menu bar and Signal, and it keeps the hour in front of me. If you're setting it up for yourself, start with [Install](install.md), and feel free to ask me anything that isn't clear.
